## Summary

A full-screen program draws on the alt screen, which has no scrollback, so the minimap has nothing to show for it. muffer's full-screen view is one, and so are vim and less. SilkTerm sees every scroll such a program makes, though. This note looks at keeping the rows that scroll off as a history of SilkTerm's own, for the map and for a read-only view of it.

The short answer is that it works for a program that scrolls its transcript, and it was measured on less, vim and nano. It can't recover rows a program jumped past without drawing them. muffer itself was not tried.

muffer's default view is on the main screen, and its transcript already goes into the real scrollback (2026101310563323). The map could show that today if muffer were on the list in `scroll.minimap.tui_process_whitelist`. Not tried.

## What the code already has

- The engine's scroll ledger in the alacritty fork records every scroll: the rows that moved, how far, and the rows that left, up to 128 between 2 frames. The alt-screen slide eases from it.

- Where a program repaints instead of scrolling, the row fingerprints find a clean shift of up to 24 rows between 2 frames.

- The slide already tells a program's chrome from its scrolling rows. A row at the edge of a scroll that the program draws again, like a pager's prompt, is held still.

- So what a history needs is already there: what moved, how far, and the rows that went off the edge.

## How a history would be built

- Keep a list of rows, adn where the program's scroll region sits in it.

- A scroll by k moves that place by k. Rows that crossed the edge are written next to the region. So are rows that scrolled past between 2 frames without being drawn in either, since the ledger kept them.

- A scroll back moves the place back. The rows that come back are matched to the ones kept, not added again.

- A frame that changed with no scroll waits until output stops for 50 ms, so a repaint caught half drawn is not written over good rows. Then it is placed by matching its rows against the list. A frame that matches nothing starts a new stretch after a gap.

- A blank row on screen writes nothing, since it may be a row not drawn yet.

- Nothing is kept until a first scroll says which rows are the transcript. A program that never scrolls, like htop, adds nothing.

## Measured

The probe is `source/src/pane/vhist_probe.rs`. It does the steps above in test code only. It reads the rows as plain text, so it says nothing about colors.

### Recorded programs

Recorded with `cicd/utility/vhist/record.py` at 100x30, scrolling through a numbered 600-line file by line, half page and page, both ways, then to the end and back to the top. "Shown" is every numbered row that was on screen after any read. "Extra" is rows kept that are not a shown row the first time: duplicates and chrome. A frame is built after every read, or at most 60 or 20 times a second with the reads in between taken together.

| Program  | Frames     | Shown | Kept | Extra
| :------- | :--------- | :---- | :--- | :-----
| less 668 | every read | 259   | 218  | 103
| less 668 | 60 a sec   | 259   | 259  | 31
| less 668 | 20 a sec   | 259   | 259  | 32
| vim.tiny | all three  | 176   | 176  | 4 or 5
| nano     | all three  | 118   | 118  | 4
| htop 3.4 | all three  | 0     | 0    | 0

- less scrolls the whole screen, its prompt row with it. Holding the edge row it draws again is what makes 60 and 20 a second whole. A frame after every read lands between the scroll and the prompt's redraw often enough that the row under the prompt is lost and the prompt is kept as a row.

- less's 31 extra rows are one page back that was placed wrong, kept a second time after line 230, and 2 rows of chrome.

- htop repaints and never shifts a block of rows, so nothing was kept, which is right.

- The probe took about 15 us a frame in an optimized build, turning the whole screen into strings each time.

### muffer's layout, made up

The unit tests replay a made-up stream with muffer's layout as a recording of 2.1.280 showed it: 2 header rows, a region scrolled with `CSI S`, and a 3-row input box under it. It adds 200 lines, goes 10 wheel notches back with `CSI T` with a pill painted over the last row, 10 forward, then an Enter that repaints the transcript 2 rows up, and a 40-row jump.

- All 266 lines that were on screen are kept, in order, with no header, box or pill rows. The 16 rows the jump passed were never drawn, and nothing can know them. Same result when every new line is a repaint rather than a scroll.

- A frame every 30 new lines on a 24-row region keeps all 200. 6 rows a frame came only from the ledger.

- A frame every 7 writes, with the wheel going both ways and repaints in the same frame, kept 260 of 266, or 256 when new lines are repaints. The ledger keeps one direction a frame and starts over when it turns, and a line printed and then repainted away inside one frame is never seen. This one is printed, not asserted.

## What breaks it

- A repaint with no scroll is fine when the shift is 24 rows or less and the rows still match. A bigger jump is a gap.

- A program scrolling back through its own history is handled by matching, and mostly right. One less page back was misplaced.

- A status line inside the scroll region is held by the edge rule, except when a frame catches it half drawn.

- Overlays and popups are drawn over rows in place. They get written into the history until the program scrolls those rows again. muffer's pill did that in the made-up run and was put right when the view went forward. A dialog that closes with no scroll would leave its rows in. Not measured on a real program. One fix is to let a change in place rewrite only the newest rows.

- A resize makes the program rewrap at the new width, so nothing matches after it. The old rows would stay as they were drawn and a new stretch starts.

- Side-by-side tmux panes are repainted by tmux. Half of each row moves, no shift is found, and nothing is kept. A full-width tmux pane scrolls with a region and works.

- A tmux window switch, or the program leaving the alt screen, starts a new stretch or drops the history.

- A program that redraws an old block differently later, like a tool output that expands, keeps its old form in the history until it is scrolled past again.

## Memory and CPU

- Rows kept as text plus color runs are about 100 to 200 bytes at 100 columns, so 10,000 rows is 1 to 2 MB a pane. The cap would be the scrollback setting.

- For the map alone, rows can go straight into the map's pixel store and the text be dropped.

- The ledger already keeps rows on the alt screen for the slide, by swap rather than copy, at 3 to 5% of parse time.

- A frame needs the fingerprints the slide already makes, plus converting the rows new to the history. The probe's 15 us is an upper bound.

- No extra GPU work. A map of N kept rows composes like N lines of scrollback.

## Clicking the map

The program owns its scroll position. SilkTerm can only ask it to move.

- Option 1: send wheel events to the program, watching the ledger to see how far each one went. Each program steps its own amount, a long jump is hundreds of events, some programs scroll whatever is under the pointer, and it types into a live program. Not recommended.

- Option 2: a read-only view that SilkTerm draws over the pane from its own rows, like tmux's copy mode. A click or drag opens it at that place. It scrolls smoothly and can be selected and copied, since it's SilkTerm's own buffer. Typing, Esc, or scrolling back to the bottom closes it. The program is never touched. Rows show as they were drawn.

- Option 2 is the one to build.

## Proposal

- First step, about a day: record muffer's full-screen view while a long session is scrolled with the wheel, and replay it through the probe.
	- Run it in a private pty with no network, as the 2.1.280 recording was made.
	- Go on if 95% or more of the rows shown are kept at 60 frames a second, with under 5% extra.
	- Stop if its wheel scroll repaints in jumps over 24 rows, or if it redraws old rows differently often enough that matching fails.

- Engine, about a day: the ledger keeps a record per direction within a frame, rather than starting over when the direction turns.

- History per pane, 3 to 4 days: rows with colors, the place tracking, the edge and settle rules, a cap, and a new stretch on a resize or a program change.

- Map, 2 to 3 days: the map reads the history while the alt screen is up, with the marker where the program's view is.

- History view, 4 to 6 days: option 2 above.

- Tests, about a day: recordings of less, vim, nano and muffer kept as fixtures.

- In all, about 2 weeks for the map with nothing on click, and 3 with the view.
